# General Sales Manager (The GSM)

## Seat
Runs the new and used sales departments: the desk, the sales managers, the floor and usually the internet team. Reports to the GM. Lives in the CRM and the desking tool, structures or approves every deal, and owns the month-end unit and gross number. A bad day: Saturday, the showroom is full, three deals are waiting on the desk, and a salesperson walks up with a customer who was "booked by the AI" for a vehicle that sold Thursday.

## How they are measured and paid
- Units retailed, new and used, against the monthly forecast and any OEM stair-step target.
- Front-end gross per unit and total gross per unit including F&I, because the deal he desks feeds the back end.
- Close rate on appointments and ups, and appointment show rate from the BDC.
- Used turn and aged inventory, often shared with the used car manager.
- Pay is typically a percentage of department gross plus unit or volume bonuses (common industry practice; plans vary by store).

## What they protect
- Gross per deal. Anything that commits a price, payment, trade value or incentive to a customer before the desk sees it is a threat.
- The salesperson's ownership of the customer. Reps are paid on units, so who "owns" an up decides who gets paid.
- CRM hygiene: lead source, assigned rep, status and notes. His pipeline reports and his forecast come from it.
- Floor time. Salespeople ignore anything that adds clicks or another app.
- Lead source attribution, because he defends marketing spend by source every month.

## How they judge a proposal
1. Does it ever quote price, payment, rate, trade value or availability without the desk? If so, who approved that?
2. When an appointment is booked, does the right salesperson know before the customer arrives, with context?
3. Is the vehicle actually in stock and not sold or in recon at the time of the appointment?
4. Will this show up in my CRM with the right lead source, rep and status, or am I reconciling by hand?
5. Does it raise show rate or close rate on appointments, not just the count of appointments set?
6. Does it cut across rep ownership or round-robin rules in a way that starts fights on the floor?
7. What does my salesperson have to do differently, and will they do it without me policing it?

## Blocks when
- The agent can commit price, payment, financing terms or trade value without a desk-approved source.
- Booked appointments do not reach the assigned rep or the CRM before the customer arrives.
- Appointments are booked on units that are sold, in transit or not front-line ready, with no inventory check.
- Lead source or rep assignment gets overwritten, breaking attribution or commission.
- Success is measured in appointments set with no view of shows, sold units or gross.

## Does not care about
- Service scheduling details, unless a service customer is a trade-in opportunity.
- Conversation tone and persona tuning beyond "does it sound like my store".
- Call duration, message counts, or how many tools the agent called.
- Integration architecture, as long as it lands in his CRM.

## Vocabulary
Up, desk, pencil, TO (turnover), be-back, show rate, close rate, front gross, back gross, PVR, round robin, lead source, aged unit, front-line ready, stair-step.

## How this person reacts (examples)
Proposal line: "Vini will confirm the customer's monthly payment estimate before booking the test drive."
Reaction: "No. Payments come off my desk. Book the visit, capture the budget, let the rep and the desk do the pencil."
Why: A payment quoted outside the desk sets an expectation that either kills gross or kills the deal.

Proposal line: "Appointments are written to the CRM as a note on the lead."
Reaction: "A note is not an appointment. My reps work from the appointment list and the task queue. If it is not a real appointment in the CRM, it does not exist on Saturday."
Why: He judges by where his floor actually looks, not by whether data was technically written.

Proposal line: "Follow-up SMS will continue for 30 days after an unsold showroom visit."
Reaction: "Whose name is on the text, and does it stop when my rep re-engages or the deal is sold?"
Why: Rep ownership and duplicate contact with an active customer are floor-level conflicts he has to referee.

## Review lens for this persona
- **Desk control**: nothing price, payment, trade or availability related reaches the customer without a desk-approved source.
- **Rep handoff**: the assigned salesperson gets the appointment and context in the CRM before arrival.
- **Inventory truth**: booked vehicles are checked against live stock and front-line status.
- **Attribution integrity**: lead source, rep assignment and status survive the integration.
- **Outcome metric**: success is shows, sold units or gross, not appointments set alone.
