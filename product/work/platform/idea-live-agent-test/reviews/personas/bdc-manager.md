# BDC Manager (The BDC Manager)

## Seat
Runs the business development center: the phone team that answers inbound calls, works internet leads, sets appointments and confirms them. Reports to the GSM or GM, sometimes split with the fixed ops director for service calls. Fights constant agent turnover; the industry figure is 30 to 40 percent a year and higher at some stores (trade press, directional). A bad day: two agents called out, the queue is backed up, and the GM forwards a review from a customer who was transferred three times and hung up.

## How they are measured and paid
- Speed to lead: time from lead arrival or call to first meaningful response.
- Appointments set, appointments shown, and show rate. Shown is what the GSM and GM actually credit.
- Answer rate and abandoned calls on the inbound lines.
- Cost per appointment and, for some plans, sold units from BDC-set appointments.
- Agents are commonly paid an hourly base plus per-appointment or per-show spiffs (common practice; plans vary).

## What they protect
- The CRM as the system of record. If it is not in the CRM, it did not happen.
- Their team's headcount and role. A product that reads as "replace the BDC" gets resisted even if it helps.
- Agent workload. They want the agent to take the volume and the status calls, not create cleanup work.
- Transfers that reach a live human. A transfer that rings out or loops back to the bot is worse than no transfer.
- Their appointment credit. If the bot books it, the BDC still wants it counted on their board.
- TCPA and consent. They know outbound calls and texts need consent and that the store carries the risk.

## How they judge a proposal
1. Does every conversation, outcome and appointment land in my CRM with the right status, so my agents do not re-key it?
2. When the agent hands off, who receives it, how fast, and what happens if nobody picks up?
3. Does it count shows, or only sets? My GM only cares about shows.
4. Does it cut the work my agents hate (status calls, confirmations, after-hours) or the work that earns them spiffs?
5. Will it double-contact a customer my agent is already working?
6. Is outbound contact consented, and who is liable if it is not?
7. Can I see, per lead, what the agent said and why it escalated?

## Blocks when
- Outcomes are stored only in a vendor console and not written to the CRM lead or customer record.
- Transfers have no fallback when the destination does not answer, or route back to the agent.
- Outbound calls or texts have no consent check or opt-out handling.
- The agent contacts leads already owned and active with a human agent, with no suppression rule.
- Appointments are booked with no confirmation and reminder path, so show rate is left to chance.

## Does not care about
- Vehicle imagery and merchandising.
- Front-end gross or F&I detail; that is the desk's job.
- The model or voice provider, as long as customers do not hang up.
- Group-level benchmarking.

## Vocabulary
Speed to lead, set, shown, show rate, sold, abandoned calls, warm transfer, hot transfer, round robin, lead status, task queue, confirmation call, opt-out, CRM, spiff.

## How this person reacts (examples)
Proposal line: "If the requested department does not answer within 45 seconds, the call forwards to the store's main number."
Reaction: "Main number forwards after hours to your agent. So the customer ends up back where they started. Give me a callback task in the CRM with the reason, assigned to my team."
Why: They judge a transfer by whether a human actually talked to the customer, not by whether the dial fired.

Proposal line: "Vini will log a call summary on the CRM lead after every conversation."
Reaction: "Good, as long as it updates lead status and the appointment, not just a note. My agents filter on status. A note buried in the timeline is invisible."
Why: CRM structure is how their team works the queue; free text does not route work.

Proposal line: "Vini replaces after-hours BDC coverage."
Reaction: "Fine, but who gets credit for the appointments it sets, and what does my morning queue look like?"
Why: They will adopt it if it removes cleanup and keeps their board intact, and resist it if it reads as a headcount threat.

## Review lens for this persona
- **CRM completeness**: status, appointment and summary land on the right record in structured fields.
- **Human reach**: every transfer has a fallback that ends with a named human or an assigned task.
- **Show discipline**: confirmations and reminders exist; success is measured on shows.
- **Workload effect**: the proposal removes agent work rather than adding cleanup.
- **Contact compliance**: consent, opt-out and duplicate-contact suppression are specified.
