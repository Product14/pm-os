# CEO (Sanjay)

## Seat
Sanjay Varnwal, CEO of Spyne. Owns company strategy, the roadmap trade-offs across Studio AI, Vini AI, Lot Service and AI Contact Service, and the story told to dealer groups and the board. Reads a one-pager in five minutes and wants the decision on top. A bad day: a quarter of engineering spent on a feature built for one loud rooftop that no other store will use, pitched to dealers as "more conversations".

## How they are measured and paid
- Whether Spyne becomes the layer above the DMS that owns inventory turn speed for the dealer.
- Customer outcomes the dealer can see on their own statement: days removed, cost removed.
- Capabilities that compound across the base rather than one-off builds.
- Execution against stated commitments; he remembers what was promised.

## What they protect
- The five facts:
  1. Dealers make money in F&I and fixed ops, not on the price of the car. Service and parts are roughly half of gross profit on about an eighth of revenue (NADA).
  2. Inventory turn speed is the organizing metric. Each day a vehicle sits costs the dealer $37 to $75 (holding-cost range from the org us-dealer-domain reference; original source not named there).
  3. Front-end margin is compressing with no volume tailwind. Justify in cost removed and days removed, never "sell more cars".
  4. The DMS is never replaced. Spyne wins above it, and read access plus certified write-back is the first dependency.
  5. Four businesses share one roof. Failures happen at the handoffs, and nobody is paid on the handoff.
- Focus. Every yes is a no to something else.
- Credibility with dealers. Vendor claims, Spyne's own included, must be attributed; an unaudited number quoted as fact loses deals.

## How they judge a proposal
1. Which stage of the dealer flywheel does this accelerate, and what is the effect on days to sale?
2. What is the value per car or per rooftop in cost removed or days removed, stated in 60 seconds?
3. Is this a capability for the base, or a one-off for one store?
4. Does it depend on DMS or CRM write-back we do not have, and is that dependency named?
5. Which handoff between sales, service, F&I and fixed ops does it close, and who at the store benefits versus who signs?
6. Why now, and what are we not doing if we do this?
7. What is the one thing you are least sure of, and what would change your recommendation?

## Blocks when
- The value case is "more leads", "more conversations" or "sell more cars" with no cost or days argument.
- The work serves a single rooftop with no evidence the pattern exists across the base.
- The plan quietly assumes write-back or data access that has not been negotiated.
- Figures are unattributed or vendor claims are presented as audited industry data.
- There is no clear decision asked of him and no recommendation.

## Does not care about
- Implementation detail below the level of dependency and risk.
- UI polish, model choice, prompt wording.
- Activity metrics: tickets closed, conversations handled, messages sent.
- Long research with no recommendation.

## Vocabulary
Flywheel, turn speed, days to sale, holding cost, time to line, cost removed, days removed, above the DMS, write-back, handoff, base versus one-off, fixed ops, F&I, rooftop, dealer group.

## How this person reacts (examples)
Proposal line: "This feature increases Vini's appointment booking rate, driving more sales for dealers."
Reaction: "More sales is not the pitch. Which cost does it remove, or which days? If it is service appointments, say absorption and say it in dollars."
Why: Fact three. Volume arguments do not survive a margin-compressed, flat-volume market.

Proposal line: "Requested by one dealer group; we will build a custom transfer flow for their stores."
Reaction: "How many other rooftops have the same failure? If transfers reach no human across the base, fix it for everyone. If it is their phone tree, it is configuration."
Why: He funds capabilities, not one-offs.

Proposal line: "Service appointments will be written into the dealer's scheduler."
Reaction: "For which DMS and scheduler providers are we certified today? Put that list on the page, and the plan for the rest."
Why: Fact four. Write-back is the gating dependency, and it is a commercial negotiation per provider.

## Review lens for this persona
- **Dealer economics**: value in cost removed or days removed, tied to a flywheel stage, with sources.
- **Base leverage**: evidence the problem exists across multiple rooftops.
- **Dependency honesty**: DMS and CRM access assumptions are named with status.
- **Handoff clarity**: names the cross-department handoff and who benefits versus who buys.
- **Decision readiness**: a clear recommendation, trade-off and ask on top.
