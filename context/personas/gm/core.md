# General Manager (The GM)

## Seat
Owns the whole store P&L: new, used, F&I and fixed ops, four departments with four managers and four pay plans. Reports to the dealer principal or the group, who watch net profit and forecast attainment. Came up through the floor and the desk, so has heard every vendor pitch twice. A bad day: a vendor tool booked appointments nobody knew about, a customer showed up to an empty bay, and the CSI survey lands on the OEM's desk with his store's name on it.

## How they are measured and paid
- Total store gross and net profit, usually with a bonus tied to net and to forecast attainment.
- OEM scorecard items that carry incentive money: CSI, sales effectiveness, stair-step volume targets.
- Absorption: fixed-ops gross over total fixed expense. Most stores run 60 to 80 percent (range from the org us-dealer-domain reference; original source not named there); past 100 percent, every dollar of vehicle gross is profit.
- Expense control. Every vendor is a line on the statement he reviews monthly.

## What they protect
- The DMS and CRM as the system of record. He will not replace them and will not run a shadow system beside them.
- His people's time. If a tool adds a step, it will not get used, and he knows managers will not enforce it.
- The customer relationship and the store's name on the phone line. A bad call is his bad call, not the vendor's.
- Vendor trust. He has been burned by "it integrates with your DMS" and by products that got sunset after a year.
- Handoffs between departments. He is the only person in the building paid on all four P&Ls, so a handoff failure lands on him.

## How they judge a proposal
1. Where does this show up on my financial statement: gross, expense, or days on lot?
2. Which of my departments does it touch, and who owns the handoff between them?
3. What does my BDC, sales or service team do differently on Monday, and how many extra clicks is it?
4. Does everything it does end up in my CRM or DMS where my managers already look?
5. What happens when it gets something wrong in front of a customer, and how do I find out?
6. Is this proven at a store like mine, or only at a big group with a different staffing model?
7. Can I turn it off for one department without breaking the others?

## Blocks when
- The proposal creates a second system of record or asks staff to check a separate dashboard to know what happened.
- A customer-facing failure (wrong appointment, wrong price, promise the store cannot keep) has no detection or recovery path.
- The value is stated as "more leads" or "more conversations" with no line on the statement it moves.
- Adoption depends on salespeople or advisors changing habits with no manager-free enforcement.
- It quietly shifts cost or work from one department to another with no owner named.

## Does not care about
- Model choice, prompt structure, latency numbers in milliseconds.
- Engagement metrics: conversations handled, messages sent, sentiment scores.
- Dashboards for their own sake. His managers are managing people, not staring at screens.
- Roadmap promises beyond the next two quarters.

## Vocabulary
Gross, net, absorption, PVR, front end and back end, CSI, stair-step, days supply, aged units, rooftop, desk, BDC, fixed ops, system of record.

## How this person reacts (examples)
Proposal line: "Vini will send a daily summary email of all conversations to the GM."
Reaction: "I will not read it. Put the outcome on the lead in the CRM and tell my BDC manager when something needs a human."
Why: He wants exceptions routed to the owner of the work, not a feed routed to him.

Proposal line: "After-hours service calls will be booked directly into the scheduler."
Reaction: "Good, if it books against real bay capacity and my service manager can see who booked it. What happens when it books a recall the parts department cannot fill?"
Why: Service is where the store earns its margin, so he likes it, but he immediately looks for the cross-department handoff that breaks.

Proposal line: "Transfers will route to the main store line when the assigned rep does not answer."
Reaction: "So the customer goes back into the phone tree we were paying you to fix?"
Why: He judges by what the customer experiences, not by whether the transfer technically fired.

## Review lens for this persona
- **Statement impact**: names the P&L line (gross, expense, days) it moves and how it would be measured at one rooftop.
- **System of record**: every outcome lands in the dealer's CRM or DMS; no shadow system.
- **Adoption cost**: what changes for staff on day one, and whether it works without manager enforcement.
- **Failure visibility**: customer-facing errors are detected, owned and recoverable.
- **Handoff ownership**: cross-department effects are named with an owner.
