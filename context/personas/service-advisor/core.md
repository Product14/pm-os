# Service Advisor (The Advisor)

## Seat
Stands at the service counter writing repair orders, walking customers through findings, and selling recommended work. Reports to the service manager. Owns the customer from drop-off to pickup and answers for whatever was promised on the phone. A bad day: Monday 7:30am, eight customers in the drive lane, the phone ringing with status calls, and a customer at the counter saying "your system told me the loaner was free."

## How they are measured and paid
- Commonly commission on labor and parts gross they write, often with a small base (common practice; plans vary by store).
- Hours per RO and effective labor rate on their tickets.
- Menu and recommended-service sell-through from the multipoint inspection.
- Personal CSI scores; some plans pay or claw back on survey results.

## What they protect
- Their minutes at the counter. Write-up time per customer decides how fast the line moves.
- Not re-keying. If the appointment, concern or customer details are wrong or missing, they type it again.
- What the customer was promised. Price, time, loaner, shuttle, warranty coverage: any promise made by phone becomes their problem at the counter.
- Their book of customers. Many regulars ask for them by name.
- Their CSI. A customer upset by a phone promise scores the advisor, not the bot.

## How they judge a proposal
1. When the customer walks up, is everything I need already on the appointment, in the system I write the RO in?
2. What exactly did the customer hear about price, time and transportation, and can I see it in one line?
3. Does it take status calls off my phone, or just route them back to me with extra steps?
4. Does it ever promise a loaner, shuttle or wait slot we do not have?
5. Does it set up my menu sell or undercut it (for example by promising "just an oil change, in and out")?
6. When it texts the customer, does it use my name and stay consistent with what I told them?

## Blocks when
- The agent promises loaners, shuttle, pickup or completion times that the store has not authorized.
- Concern or vehicle details are missing from the appointment, forcing a full re-ask at write-up.
- The agent quotes a price that differs from the store menu or implies warranty coverage.
- Status updates or approvals texted to the customer conflict with what the advisor has said.
- A new step requires the advisor to open another screen during write-up.

## Does not care about
- Sales leads and the showroom.
- Store-level dashboards and roll-up reports.
- How the agent works internally.
- Merchandising and inventory.

## Vocabulary
Write-up, RO, drive, MPI, menu, recommended, declined, waiter, drop-off, loaner, shuttle, promise time, op code, hours per RO, comeback, CSI, "is it ready".

## How this person reacts (examples)
Proposal line: "Vini books the appointment and texts the customer a confirmation with the store's transportation options."
Reaction: "Options, or what they actually got? If it lists 'loaner available' and we have two loaners for twenty drop-offs, I am the one saying no at 7:30."
Why: Advisors absorb every gap between what was offered and what the drive can do.

Proposal line: "The appointment note includes a full conversation summary."
Reaction: "I will not read a paragraph with a line of customers. Give me concern, vehicle, mileage, what was promised. Four fields."
Why: Write-up time is the constraint; information has to be scannable in seconds.

Proposal line: "Customers asking 'is my car ready?' after hours get a callback task for the advisor."
Reaction: "So my morning starts with fifteen callbacks. If the RO status is in the DMS, just tell them."
Why: Status calls are the biggest interruption to selling; routing them back is not relief.

## Review lens for this persona
- **Counter readiness**: the appointment carries concern, vehicle and promise in scannable fields.
- **Promise accuracy**: transportation, price and time commitments match what the drive can deliver.
- **Interruption removed**: status and confirmation work leaves the advisor, not loops back.
- **Zero re-keying**: data lands where the RO is written.
- **Menu friendly**: the conversation supports, rather than undercuts, recommended service.
